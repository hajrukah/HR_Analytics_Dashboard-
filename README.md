# 📊 HR Analytics Dashboard

An HR Analytics project built with **Microsoft Excel** to analyze employee demographics, compensation, job roles, overtime, job satisfaction, and workforce characteristics.

## 📌 Project Overview

This project explores an HR dataset to transform raw employee information into meaningful workforce insights.

The analysis focuses on understanding employee distribution, income, overtime, job roles, marital status, job satisfaction, years at the company, and departmental patterns.

The goal was to demonstrate how Excel can be used to **clean, analyze, summarize, and visualize HR data to support data-driven decision-making**.

## 🎯 Project Objectives

* Analyze the overall employee population
* Examine employee income and compensation patterns
* Identify job roles with higher overtime counts
* Compare employee distribution across departments
* Analyze marital status distribution
* Examine job satisfaction across age groups
* Analyze years employees have spent at the company
* Explore gender-based income patterns
* Build an interactive HR dashboard for communicating insights

## 🔎 Key Findings

### 1. Employee distribution varies considerably by department

Life Sciences represents the largest employee group in the dataset, followed by Marketing and Medical. Human Resources has the smallest employee count among the departments shown.

This highlights a workforce structure that is concentrated in a few major departments.

### 2. Overtime is concentrated in specific job roles

Sales Executive and Research Scientist recorded the highest overtime counts among the job roles analyzed, while Human Resources and several other roles recorded substantially lower overtime counts.

This suggests that overtime workload is not evenly distributed across the organization.

### 3. Monthly income differs across departments

Life Sciences accounts for the largest share of monthly income at approximately **41%**, followed by Medical at approximately **32%**.

The remaining departments contribute smaller portions of the overall monthly income.

### 4. Gender income distribution shows a difference

The analysis recorded approximately **5.64 million** in monthly income for male employees compared with approximately **3.93 million** for female employees.

This difference should be interpreted alongside workforce composition and other relevant employee characteristics rather than treated automatically as evidence of a pay gap.

### 5. Job satisfaction varies across age groups

Employees aged **26–35** recorded the highest total job satisfaction score in the analysis, followed by employees aged **36–45**.

The 55+ group recorded the lowest total satisfaction score among the age categories shown.

### 6. Employee tenure varies across the workforce

The years-at-company analysis shows different levels of accumulated employee tenure, with employees having between 2 and 5 years at the company contributing substantially to the total years represented in the analysis.

This provides a useful basis for examining retention and employee experience.

## 💡 Recommendations

### 1. Review overtime concentration

HR should investigate why certain roles, particularly Sales Executive and Research Scientist positions, record higher overtime levels.

Workload balancing, staffing reviews, and resource allocation could help reduce excessive overtime.

### 2. Monitor employee satisfaction

The lower satisfaction levels observed among older employees should encourage HR to investigate factors affecting engagement and satisfaction within different age groups.

Employee feedback and engagement surveys can help identify the underlying causes.

### 3. Investigate income differences

The observed difference in total monthly income between male and female employees should be investigated alongside employee count, job roles, seniority, and other compensation-related factors.

This can help HR distinguish workforce-composition effects from potential compensation disparities.

### 4. Strengthen retention strategies

The tenure analysis can be used to identify stages where employees may be more likely to leave.

HR can introduce targeted engagement, career-development, recognition, and retention initiatives based on employee tenure.

### 5. Use workforce data for planning

The differences in employee distribution and income contribution across departments can support workforce planning, budgeting, recruitment, and resource allocation decisions.
